## Deviations from the contract
- `link()` gains `tau=0.5` and `frames=None`. The unary needs logit(tau), and the contract signature has no way to pass it; `select_ball` passes the events tau.
- Restart rule in the linker. A gap longer than 5 detection frames ends the chain, and the next one may start anywhere with no motion term. Every frame without a ball costs c_null, including frames between chains. The spec doesn't say what happens beyond a 5-frame skip, so this is my reading.
- Tau and top-k are applied after the grass and outline filters, just before the selection.
- `swap_ball_rows` drops ball rows at frames the pipeline didn't detect on (for example `--extra-frames`). A ball sample in a frame without players would read as a gap in possession. Copying the source unchanged (`rows=None`) also copies its grass fractions.
- When `tracks_grass.parquet` is absent, `merged_from_cached` measures the grass fractions from the clip and caches them. That decodes the window once.
- Stage D's "most permissive" is made concrete as: the most shots among settings with at most 3, then fewest changes from v1, then grid order. Shots are counted as `shot` events only.
- "Fewest changes from v1" counts against top-k 4 with `select_single_ball`, which is what the published heatmap events run used. c_null and c_static only count when the linker is selected.

**Deviations from the contract**
1. **One 6-channel convolution.** `conv1_motion` keeps its own parameter (`conv1_motion.weight`, separate learning rate), but the forward pass runs one convolution with `cat([conv1.weight, conv1_motion.weight], 1)`. That is the same sum before bn1, at 1.06× v1 instead of 1.15–1.20× for two convolutions.
   - Zero motion through the 6-channel path matches the RGB net to about 1e-6.
   - A 3-channel input to the motion model is exact zero motion; `motion_zero=True` uses it.
2. **Background centre** rounds half up: t = 25 mod 50 goes to the next multiple. This is consistent as long as everyone calls `bg_centre`.
3. **`ready(t)`** is true once decoding has passed the last sample frame, or after `finish()`. A sample that was never added counts as missing, so decode gaps are tolerated. It is the same as the contract when every frame is added.
4. **`get(t)` raises** if called before `ready(t)` or after its samples were dropped, instead of returning a background from fewer samples.
5. **Even-count median** is the mean of the middle two, rounded half up. The median uses a sorting network: about 0.08 s for 15 × 1000×4096, against 0.6–1.6 s with numpy or torch.
6. **`check_unseen` also raises when the checkpoint has no provenance**, unless `allow_seen`.
7. **Provenance format:** `"train"` is required as {match: [halves]}; `"exclude"` (V2_EXCLUDE) is stored as rows [match, half, first, last]. `write_provenance` refuses any NEVER match.
8. **`load_calibration`** also accepts a `.json` path directly.
9. **Shared input buffer:** `detect_v2` builds its input in one module-level buffer reused between calls (about 100 MB kept alive). The input tensor is only valid until the next call, and it is not thread-safe.
10. **`motion_channels`** takes optional `sigma_d` / `sigma_f`; pass 1.0 to get the raw differences.

**Deviations from the contract and why**
1. **Colour augmentation.** Saturation is a blend with the grey image instead of v1's HSV scaling, so luminance — all the motion channels see — is unchanged. Brightness, contrast and saturation are one affine map (one `cv2.transform`), centred on the t crop's mean, applied identically to all four crops. The grey background gets only brightness and contrast, which is exact up to rounding. With HSV, the stored grey background would no longer match the frame and F would carry a spurious offset.
2. **Noise.** It is luminance noise (the same value on B, G and R), independent on each of t-2, t and t+2, with its own sigma drawn from 0–2. The background gets none: it is a median of 15 frames, and t's noise already reaches F.
3. **Scale.** The factor is drawn log-uniform in 0.8–1.25 and rounded to whole cells. When cropping back to 320 px the bag is kept inside where it fits; when padding, the image is reflected and the codes there are 0.
4. **Drop-worst rounds at random.** With 3 action bags and 1 dribble bag per batch, ordinary rounding would never drop either (0.45 and 0.25 bags). Random rounding gives the stated 25/15/25% on average. Drops start after step 1200, matching v1's `> 1000`.
5. **Missing neighbours and backgrounds** are read as `None`, using `has_prev`, `has_next` and `n_bg`, so the motion is exactly zero as at inference. The copies of t stored in those slots would carry JPEG and noise differences.
6. **Abort** fires at any monitor from step 1500 on (1500 included), in both `--motion` and `--no-motion`. Peakiness is pooled over flight, action and dribble bags; per-kind values are printed too.
7. **Provenance `train`** lists the matches and halves actually present in `labels.parquet`, checked against `V2_TRAIN`, rather than copying `V2_TRAIN`. Label constants come from `labels_meta.json` (what the codes were made with), with a `label_constants_as_code` flag and a log note if they differ from the current `label_ball_strips.py`. Extras: file and label hashes, the mix, learning rates, augmentation settings, monitor history and counts per kind.
8. **Additions not in the contract:**
   - a prefetch thread for batch preparation (batches stay deterministic);
   - `--bench` with `--motion` also times the RGB-only step on the same batches, interleaved, and prints the ratio;
   - `--bench-at` picks the schedule step to start from;
   - the log prints the mean loss over the last 100 steps.

**Open issues for other components**
- **Sigmas:** `SIGMA_D` and `SIGMA_F` in `src/ball_heatmap.py` are still the 0.02 / 0.05 placeholders. The trainer records whatever is there at the time, so they must be edited before the real run. C's smoke `labels_meta.json` predates C's new `sigma` key, so the trainer prints a note.
- **Clipping (B's question):** nothing in training clips the heavy-tailed D channel. If clipping is wanted, it belongs in `motion_bg` so training and inference match.
- **A / E — RGB-only checkpoints:** a `--no-motion` checkpoint has no `conv1_motion.weight`. It loads strictly into `build()`, or into `build(motion=True)` with `strict=False`; `detect_v2(motion_zero=True)` then gives exact RGB results. Its provenance has `"motion": false` so loaders can tell.
- **Go/no-go:** the timings above are 3–4× inflated by other jobs. Re-run `--bench 20` and `--bench 20 --bench-at 801` on an idle CPU with the real strips dir (`.cache/soccertrack_v2/ball_strips` does not exist yet). The frozen-phase ratio is where the gate can fail.
- **Monitor cost:** it re-reads the 300 held-out strips every 250 steps, about 19 forward passes; I estimate about 10 s each time on an idle machine.
